## Abstract

Live-cell perturbation experiments often contain dynamic control information that is lost when trajectories are reduced to endpoints, amplitudes, or generic time-series features. RhoDyn is a computational method for residence-state inference that scores dwell within user-declared biological windows, compares amplitude and residence summaries, evaluates bounded coupling under declared margins, constructs measurement-scoped reserve-like endpoint summaries, and tests routed-output alternatives against reduced architectures. Across synthetic truth cases, public calcium and ERK signaling trajectories, public-derived endpoint demonstrations, and held-out coupling contexts, RhoDyn exposes cases in which residence, buffering, coupling boundaries, or routed outputs change interpretation relative to simpler summaries while preserving inconclusive regimes. The Python, CLI, backend, workbench, and archived release surfaces produce matched, inspectable outputs. RhoDyn therefore provides a reproducible route for identifying dynamic operating-state structure in live-cell perturbation data without treating every signal window, endpoint coordinate, or effective model term as a literal mechanism.
